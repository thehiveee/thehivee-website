The Real Problem

You’re Paying for Ads. Can You Name the Jobs They Brought In?

The report shows up every month. Clicks, impressions, a line going up. Then you look at the schedule and try to work out which of those jobs came from the ads. Usually nobody can tell you.

That’s because getting a job from an ad takes four things, and most of the attention goes to one. The ad brings in the right homeowner. The website turns the click into a call. The tracking shows which calls were real. The offer gets the yes.

Break any one of them and you’re paying for clicks that never ring. A slow site loses the homeowner. No tracking and you can’t tell a customer from a spam call. A weak offer and they phone the next company on the list.

So I build and run all four for every client. Myself.

Why It Matters

A Lead Isn’t the Finish Line. A Booked Job Is.

An agency can hit every lead number in the report while your calendar stays half empty. On paper, they did their job. Whether you made any money is left to you.

I don’t count that as a win. If the phone’s ringing and the bookings aren’t following, I’ll look at what the homeowner hears when they call: your price, your guarantee, your promotion. Whatever is losing you the job.

I can’t answer the phone or close the job for you. That part is your team’s. My job is to make sure the right homeowner calls, and that you have an offer worth saying yes to when they do.

Clicks don’t pay your techs. Booked jobs do. That’s the number I’m working for.
